Capstone Reflection

My road into data started with basketball. As a kid I was always researching players and trying to quantify how good they really were. I wanted more than the score and basic statlines. That curiosity led into school, where I spent my time messing around in Python with sports data. I did not know it then, but sports were only the doorway.

What made me stay was how much numbers can explain. When the NFL started using Next Gen Stats, I was fascinated. Something that happens in a split second on the field is measured, processed, and put in front of me instantly. That left me with a feeling that has never gone away: numbers are a beautiful way to explain what is really happening, and they uncover hidden secrets in all facets of the world.

Once I saw data that way, I could not keep it inside sports. The same thinking applies almost anywhere, and I have always been talented in math, so I keep looking for new places to use that talent. Sometimes that means building a dashboard based on my friends’ group chat data to see who is actually the funniest. Other times it means developing models to help make better decisions. The subject changes every time, and I'm always enjoying it.

My favorite thing to do is take a popular opinion/assumptions and test whether it holds up using numbers. I enjoy finding a creative way to ask a question, and I like that the data does not care what is popular. Sometimes it confirms the crowd, and sometimes it shows something better.

Data science and mathematics let me keep following that curiosity wherever it leads. Sports opened the door, and what I found on the other side is a world of data much bigger than anything I imagined as a kid.
